### C1. **Public repo is missing the headline checkpoints** (reproducibility breach)

**The actual PFWL3S checkpoints — the ones that produce the abstract's 4-strict-CI claim — are NOT in the public repo.** They were on the RunPod network volume but only the Triad-distill subset was pulled. The headline reproducibility claim is therefore false as written.

**Fix:** spin the pod back up (or check if the volume is still mounted), pull the missing `tierC1/pathfinder_*` directories, commit. Probably ~50-200 MB of additional ckpts. The §5.13 PFWL3S-d=5-seed{0,1,2,3,4} ckpts and d=9-seed{0,1,2} ckpts are similarly missing.

**Severity:** any reviewer who actually tries to reproduce the headline strict-CI claim will fail. This is by far the most consequential finding.

### C2. **Lange in/out-of-distribution comparison is buried** (fairness disclosure)

The paper's strict-CI Lange-beating claims are at **p ∈ {0.007, 0.010, 0.015}** at d=5, d=7 (abstract line 10). Lange et al.'s open weights were trained at **p ∈ {0.001, …, 0.005}** only (line 14 priority note; §5.11 line 430). So the headline wins are achieved by:

- Pathfinder: **deliberately fine-tuned at p=0.007** (single-noise specialization, §4.5 + §5.11 line 411)
- Lange: **evaluated out-of-distribution at p=0.007, 0.010, 0.015** with no retraining

This is an in-distribution-vs-OOD comparison, not in-distribution-vs-in-distribution. A reviewer at Google/MIT would immediately ask: *"What happens if you fine-tune Lange's GNN on p=0.007 using the same recipe? Or train both decoders on identical data?"* The paper does not answer this. §5.11 line 430 says "the full 21-point sweep extending to p ∈ {0.001, 0.002, 0.015} is in `bench/results/h200_lange_headtohead_{low,high}_p.json`; the qualitative conclusion is the same" — but Lange is still OOD at p=0.015.

The current abstract phrasing — "strictly beats Lange's individual LER" (line 10) — implies a fair head-to-head. The actual claim is more limited: *"strictly beats Lange's published p ≤ 0.005-trained weights when evaluated OOD at p ≥ 0.007"*. That's a substantively weaker statement and should be flagged explicitly.

**Fix options:**
- (Best) Fine-tune Lange's GNN at p=0.007 using their published training code; re-run the comparison. ~$30-50 GPU. If Lange-finetuned still loses to PFWL3S → strong claim. If it ties or wins → revise abstract.
- (Minimum) Add a paragraph to the abstract and §5.11 explicitly stating that Lange is OOD at the headline noise points and that no retraining of Lange was attempted; note this as a limitation in §6.3.

**Severity:** the entire "first individual decoder to strictly beat Lange's GNN" framing depends on this. Reviewers will not let this go.

### C3. **Abstract is ~800 words; standard limit is 250-300**

Lines 10-14 contain four paragraphs totaling roughly 800 words. Lines 10 alone is a single ~600-word run-on sentence with 11 distinct numerical comparisons. No academic venue will accept this. The abstract has become the catch-all for all the result expansions over the project lifetime.

**Fix:** rewrite to ~300 words. Lead with the two headline systems (Pathfinder, Pathfinder-Triad), state the strict-CI win count and the latency claim, end with the negative-result that strengthens the Triad case. Move per-rate LER tables to §5.13.

**Severity:** desk-reject level at most venues.

## MAJOR — should fix before submission

### M1. **The "12× faster than Lange" claim is for single-seed Pathfinder, not the PFWL3S system that produces the strict-CI win**

Abstract line 10: *"the 3-seed-average inference cost is exactly 3× the single-model cost (measured: 157.36 μs/syn at d=7 B=1024 without Triton, ≈24 μs/syn with Triton extrapolated…)"*. So PFWL3S without Triton is **slower than Lange** (157 vs 71.67 μs/syn). With the §5.3 Triton kernel applied per-ckpt (which is theoretical — not actually measured for PFWL3S at H=384), ~24 μs/syn — 3× faster than Lange, but well over the 7-μs cycle budget.

§1 contribution #2 (line 35): *"Pathfinder + Triton is the only open-source decoder tested that sustains the d=7 7-μs cycle-time budget."* This is true for **canonical (H=256, 1-seed) Pathfinder + Triton at 6.12 μs/syn**, not for PFWL3S, not for Pathfinder-Triad. The latency-winning configuration and the LER-winning configuration are different systems, but the abstract's framing blurs them.

**Fix:** Explicitly disambiguate in the abstract:
- Cycle-time-budget claim → canonical 1-seed Pathfinder + Triton (LER 3.34% at d=7 p=0.007, loses to Lange individually).
- Strict-CI-win claim → PFWL3S (LER 2.49% at d=7 p=0.007, off-budget at ~24 μs/syn with Triton extrapolated).
- Pathfinder-Triad → ~72 μs/syn, way off-budget, lowest LER.

These are three distinct accuracy/latency operating points. The paper should ship a single sentence per system and let the reader pick.

### M2. **The "Pathfinder-Triad" LER moves between sections**

Tracking the number quoted as "Pathfinder-Triad LER" at d=7 p=0.007:

| Source | LER | PF voter used |
|---|---|---|
| Abstract line 12 | 2.45% | canonical fine-tune |
| §5.12 Table 10 line 492 | 2.417% | canonical fine-tune |
| §5.12 100K-shot confirmation (line 502) | 2.454% | canonical fine-tune |
| §5.13 Wide-Long table (line 577) | 2.470% | Wide-Long single-seed |
| §5.13 PFWL3S table (line 593) | 2.384% | PFWL3S 3-seed |

| README.md line 6 | 2.42% | unclear which |

The paper acknowledges the moving target (§5.13 line 583 says *"reaffirming the ensemble is robust to the choice of the canonical Pathfinder voter"*), but it would help reviewers if the abstract picked ONE number and named the voter, with a footnote pointing to the others. Currently the abstract says 2.45% (with canonical fine-tune voter) but lines 593 & 564 highlight 2.384% / 2.399% (with PFWL3S / mega voters) as "the lowest open-source LER reported in this paper." Reviewers will ask "which Pathfinder-Triad is THE Pathfinder-Triad?"

**Fix:** abstract should quote 2.384% (the lowest, with PFWL3S voter) and note that this is the system reported in §5.13's headline result. Or pick 2.45% (canonical voter) for the §5.12 vintage and clearly distinguish "the original Pathfinder-Triad" vs "the PFWL3S Pathfinder-Triad."

### M3. **Compute cost claim is stale by ~3×**

Total actual cost is **~$300-400**, not $100.

**Fix:** rewrite §4.6 to reflect the full exploration cost. Either give an honest total ("approximately $300 over six weeks of elapsed time including the Triad-distill arc reported in §5.13") or restrict the $100 figure to the original Table 1 + Triton work and add a separate per-section cost breakdown.

### M5. **§5.11 Lange-head-to-head and §5.12 Triad use *different* PF checkpoints under the same name**

§5.11 line 411 says: *"This is the same recipe at every distance — one script, three checkpoints (`finetune_d3`, `finetune_d5`, `finetune_d7`). Every 'Pathfinder' row in the table below is one of these checkpoints."*

§5.12 line 463 says: *"Raw data: `bench/results/h200_main/phase2/ensemble_results_final.json` for d=3 and d=5, `bench/results/h200_main/tuned/ensemble_results_tuned.json` for d=7 (both produced by the same `ensemble_pf_lange.py` harness; different files because the d=7 row uses the `finetune_d7` checkpoint specifically whereas phase2's d=7 row used `distill_d7`; this distinction is discussed in §5.13)."*

So §5.12 d=7 uses `finetune_d7` (matches §5.11) but the d=3 and d=5 ensemble rows reference a phase2 JSON whose PF voter at those distances is unclear. A reviewer auditing the data trail will get confused.

**Fix:** add a clarifying sentence to §5.12 explicitly listing the PF voter ckpt at every distance in Table 10, and verify the d=3 and d=5 phase2 numbers are with `finetune_d{3,5}` (not `distill_d{3,5}`, which would change the story).

### M6. **§6.3 says PFWL3S at d=9 "is significantly worse than Lange individually" — but PFWL3S d=9 was H=256, not H=384**

Line 776 says: *"PFWL3S at d=9 is **significantly worse than Lange individually**."* But §6.3 line 761 also says: *"A subsequent d=9 PFWL3S extension — three independent random-seed Wide-Long-style ckpts (**H=256** to match the existing d=9 chain, …)"*.

So the d=9 "PFWL3S" is actually H=256, not the H=384 used at d=5 and d=7. Calling both "PFWL3S" is misleading. The d=9 result of "PFWL3S loses to Lange individually" is therefore a result about **H=256 Triad-distilled multi-seed, not the H=384 architecture that defines PFWL3S elsewhere**.

**Fix:** rename the d=9 variant to "PFWL3S-d9-H256" or similar, and explicitly state that an H=384 d=9 variant was not trained due to the warm-init chain incompatibility. This affects the "architectural-reversal does not extend to d=9" claim — it might extend with H=384, untested.

### M7. **§5.6 ensembling analysis was done with the *narrow* H=128 distilled student, not the canonical model**

Table 5 (§5.6, line 316-319) uses "the distilled narrow H=128 student paired with PyMatching". The earlier text (line 303) describes the failure-mode disjointness using **canonical Pathfinder** (H=256). The ensemble analysis then *switches model* without flagging it. A careful reviewer will catch this and ask why.

**Fix:** either redo §5.6 Table 5 with the canonical H=256 model (the one used everywhere else), or explicitly justify why the narrow H=128 was used. The 0.01% syndrome overlap claim should also be re-verified at H=256 since the narrow model is provably less accurate.

### M10. **Triton kernel cross-validation runs were against `finetune_d7` only**

§5.3 lines 269-274 (numerical equivalence table) report "0–21 disagreements per 20,000 syndromes" measured on `finetune_d7`. This is the canonical (H=256) ckpt. The PFWL3S ckpts are at H=384 — the Triton kernel's weight-packing layout was not re-validated at H=384. The claim in §5.13 line 597 — "with the §5.3 Triton DirectionalConv3d kernel applied to each of the three forward passes, single-model latency would drop from 52.5 μs to ≈8 μs/syn (extrapolating the 6.5× Triton speedup measured in §5.3 at H=256...)" — is an extrapolation, not a measurement.

**Fix:** Either (a) re-run the Triton numerical equivalence at H=384 and report it, (b) explicitly flag the H=384 PFWL3S latency as estimated-not-measured (it currently uses "extrapolated" — which is good — but the abstract's "12× faster" claim should make this clearer), or (c) drop the PFWL3S latency forecast.

## MINOR — nice to have

### m1. **Inconsistent naming: PFWL3S vs Pathfinder-Wide-Long-3seed vs "the 3-seed-avg variant"**

The acronym is used in some places (line 561, 564), the full name in others (line 596), and the descriptive phrase in still others. Pick one and use it.

### m2. **Stim noise model parameters never defined**

§5.1 calls the canonical Pathfinder a "3-parameter" trained model and §5.11 introduces "4-parameter noise" without ever listing the 4 parameters. They're listed in the appendix (line 933 has the canonical Stim invocation) but a reviewer reading §5.1-§5.13 won't know what the 3-vs-4 distinction is. Add one sentence in §2.1 or §4.1 defining the 4 parameters (`after_clifford_depolarization`, `before_measure_flip_probability`, `after_reset_flip_probability`, `before_round_data_depolarization`) and which one canonical Pathfinder omits (`after_reset_flip_probability`).

### m3. **§5.3 line 230: "10.3× faster than AlphaQubit on TPU"**

AlphaQubit's published number (Bausch et al., Nature 2024) is for a different decoder architecture, on TPU v5, at d=5 or d=7 with measurement-error noise. The comparison is rhetorically valid but the conditions are quite different. A reviewer will want a footnote acknowledging that this is a cross-system/cross-task comparison, not a controlled benchmark.

### m4. **§5.7 phenom-noise retraction**

§5.7 line 328: "earlier versions of this work claimed Pathfinder generalizes to phenomenological noise, but a systematic eval ... contradicts that claim." Good honesty. But for a published paper, the "earlier versions" framing is internal — readers won't have seen earlier versions. Rewrite to "Naively, one might expect a CNN decoder trained on circuit-level noise to generalize to phenomenological noise; the data show this expectation is wrong" or similar.

### m5. **"Statistically significant" vs "non-overlapping 95% Wilson CIs"**

The paper consistently uses non-overlapping 95% Wilson CIs as the threshold for "stat-sig". This is a *more conservative* test than a 2-sample test for proportions (Fisher's exact, χ², or normal approximation), so the paper is under-claiming where it could over-claim. That's good practice, but a brief mention of this choice (e.g., in §5.1 methodology paragraph or a footnote on Table 1) would head off reviewer questions about why non-overlapping CIs and not a formal test of difference.

### m6. **Acknowledgments name "the Conductor Quantum team" without affiliation context**

Line 831 thanks "The Conductor Quantum team" — for transparency, add a one-line note about the relationship (collaborator, prior employer, sponsor, etc.). Reviewers value disclosed conflicts of interest.

### m7. **Reference [16] (NVIDIA Ising-Decoder, April 2026) — is it relevant enough to discuss as related work?**

§7 line 805 introduces NVIDIA's pre-decoder. If it beats PyMatching at d=13 (cited), it's potentially a stronger baseline than Lange for the "MWPM-beating open-source decoder" claim. Either compare against it briefly (or explain why a direct comparison was out of scope — e.g., different evaluation regime), or downplay.

### m8. **References use mixed citation formats**

[8] (Gu et al.) is arXiv-only and lists year 2026 — which is correct for the arxiv version but the paper hasn't appeared at a venue. Some refs have DOIs ([5] Nature, [14] PRR), others don't. Standardize.

### m9. **`run_*.py` top-level scripts are inconsistent**

`run_final_eval.py`, `run_100k_eval.py`, `run_d7_p015.py`, `run_mixed_noise.py`, `run_comprehensive_eval.py`, `run_code_types.py` are 6 scripts that overlap in purpose (all run evaluations) but with no clear hierarchy. Consolidate or add a top-level `evaluate.py` with subcommands.

### m10. **`bench/results/h200_main/`** is the de facto live working directory, exposed publicly

The repo path implicitly names "session3" of a development series. Reviewers looking at the directory structure see "session3" with subdirectories `phase2/`, `phase3/`, `phase4/`, `phase5/`, `tierA/`, `tierB/`, `tierBC/`, `tierC1/`, `tuned/`, `distill/`, `hybrid/`, `triad_distill/` — internal development history exposed. Either flatten/rename for the release ("`bench/results/`") or document the structure in a README.
