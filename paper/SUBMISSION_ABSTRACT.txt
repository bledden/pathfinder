I present two open-source decoder systems for quantum error correction on rotated surface codes, both built on Pathfinder, a direction-specific 3D CNN trained with the Muon optimizer. System 1 (canonical Pathfinder + a custom Triton kernel): a single 500K-parameter checkpoint per code distance reaches 6.43 us/syndrome at d=7, batch=1024 on an NVIDIA H200 -- batched throughput below a 7-us-per-syndrome service-rate target at every operational noise rate -- while batch-1 latency (215 us) remains far above streaming real-time requirements. On accuracy it is never statistically beaten by PyMatching across 24 (distance, noise) points under 3-parameter circuit-level noise (20 point-estimate wins, 12 with non-overlapping 95% Wilson CIs), using one fixed, validation-selected checkpoint per distance; under the 4-parameter noise model of Lange et al. it is statistically indistinguishable from PyMatching and trails Lange's GNN by ~14% relative. System 2 (Pathfinder-Triad): a 3-way majority vote of PFWL3S (a wider, 3-seed, distillation-trained Pathfinder variant), Lange's GNN, and PyMatching exploits the decoders' complementary failure modes to reach 2.384% logical error rate at d=7, p=0.007 (100K shots) -- below each component decoder, with non-overlapping CIs versus published Lange -- and strict-CI wins at five (d,p) points across d=7 and d=9. In the strongest controlled comparison (designated post hoc as primary), standalone PFWL3S beats an equal-size three-seed fine-tuned-Lange ensemble at d=7, p=0.010 under the paired McNemar test, surviving Holm and whole-paper Bonferroni correction. All accuracy results are simulation benchmarks on memory experiments; on real IBM Heron r2 data, decoded offline (outside the control loop), PFWL3S shows no statistically resolved difference from PyMatching -- a null, not parity. Six negative results are documented. Code, checkpoints, and raw data are released.
